{# ================= SECTION 1 — WHAT IS A MEMO ================= #}

What is a memo?

A one-way formal declaration that goes through a chain of approval.

A memo is different from a message: the sender writes it, addresses it, and it's delivered. It's not a conversation — replies go through the regular Messages app.

1
Draft The sender writes it. Nobody sees it yet.
2
Approval Each approver can approve, return, or reject.
3
Delivered Lands in every recipient's inbox.
{# ================= SECTION 2 — THE BUILDING BLOCKS ================= #}

The three building blocks

Routes, Steps, and Rules — that's the whole system.

{# --- ROUTES --- #}

1. Routes

A named sequence of approval steps — like a template.

"Staff memo → Division Head → Director → ES" is a route. You create it once and reuse it whenever the same pattern applies.

  • Each route has its own same-rank behaviour.
  • An empty route means "no approval — publish directly."
  • Routes are shared by rules — one route can serve many rules.
{# --- STEPS --- #}

2. Steps

One hop in a route. Each declares who acts.

Six kinds of approver are available:

division_head The sender's division head director The sender's department director deputy The sender's deputy director es The Executive Secretary specific_user A named person role_in_department Anyone with a role in a dept

Each step has two important toggles:

Optional

If ticked, this step is silently skipped when no one matches. If unticked and no one matches, the memo parks and admins are notified.

Hierarchical

If on, this step obeys the rank system (junior approvers are skipped). If off, this is a mandatory gate that always runs — even if the sender outranks the approver.

{# --- RULES --- #}

3. Rules

Match a sender + target to a route.

A rule says: "When this kind of sender writes to this kind of target, use that route."

  • Empty sender roles = matches any sender.
  • When several rules match, the one with the higher priority wins.
  • Recipient roles only matter for the "One specific person" target kind.
Example
director writing to director → Peer directors route
{# ================= SECTION 3 — RANK BEHAVIOUR ================= #}

Rank behaviour

Who outranks whom, and what happens.

Each Role has a rank number. Higher = more senior. Superusers rank 1000. When a memo advances through its chain, the system compares each approver's rank to the sender's:

↓
Junior

Approver's rank is lower than the sender's.

Step skipped
↑
Senior

Approver's rank is higher.

Acts on it
=
Same rank

Approver is a peer.

Depends on the route
✕
Self

Approver is the sender.

Always skipped
Hierarchical toggle matters. Rank rules only apply to steps with Hierarchical on. Turn it off for steps that must always run regardless of who's involved — ES sign-off, records filing, etc.

When the approver is the same rank as the sender:

skip

The step is bypassed. Good for peer-to-peer memos that don't need a signature.

keep

The peer approver still acts on it — useful for "FYI" acknowledgements between equals.

{# ================= SECTION 4 — BROADCAST PERMISSIONS ================= #}

Broadcast permissions

Who can address a whole group at once.

Everyone can:

  • Send to one specific person
  • Send to a hand-picked list

Broadcast senders can also:

  • Send to All Staff
  • Send to All Directors
  • Send to a whole department
Who has it by default? The Executive Secretary and every Superuser. Grant it to individual Directors through the Permissions page. Grants take effect immediately — no logout needed.
{# ================= SECTION 5 — WALKTHROUGH ================= #}

Setting up your memo system

Five steps. Fifteen minutes. Done.

1

Create the routes you actually need

For a typical ICT Directorate, three routes cover almost everything:

Staff to ES staff-to-es Division Head → Director → ES
Director to ES director-to-es ES
Peer directors peer-directors (empty — direct publish)
2

Wire the rules that pick them

One rule per sender/target combination:

Sender Target Route Priority
staff roles One specific person Staff to ES 10
staff roles A list of people Staff to ES 10
director One specific person (recipient = director) Peer directors 20
director One specific person (recipient = ES) Director to ES 30
director All staff Director to ES 15
executive-secretary Any Peer directors 100
3

Grant broadcast permissions

ES and Super Admins already have it. Grant it to the Directors you trust with org-wide announcements.

Go to Permissions page
4

Test before you go live

Use the Test page with three or four sample combinations. Make sure each one shows the route you expect.

Open the tester
5

Let people use it

Real memos will now flow. If someone complains about the wrong route, come back to Rules and adjust the priority or the sender-role match. Nothing requires a code change.

{# ================= SECTION 6 — GOTCHAS ================= #}

Common gotchas

What people usually run into, and how to fix it.

"My memo published without any approval."

No rule matched the sender/target combination, so the memo published directly. Go to Rules and add or adjust one. Check sender roles (empty list = any sender) and the target kind.

"The memo jumped straight to the ES."

Two knobs affect this: same-rank behaviour and the Hierarchical toggle. A step with Hierarchical on gets skipped if the approver is junior or same-ranked with the sender (and the route says skip).

"The ES step never ran."

The ES step's Hierarchical toggle should be off. Otherwise, if a senior user (or the ES themselves) sends the memo, the step gets skipped.

"A memo is stuck and no one's acting."

A required step has no resolvable approver. The memo parked itself and superusers were notified. Fix by either assigning a role/user to that step, or marking the step Optional.

"Two rules match and I don't know which one wins."

The one with the higher priority number wins. Use increments of 5 or 10 so you can slot new rules between existing ones without renumbering.

"A Director can't send to All Staff."

Broadcast permission is missing. Go to Permissions and grant them send_memo_broadcast. The ES and superusers have it automatically.
